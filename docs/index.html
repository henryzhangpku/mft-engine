mft-engine

Reasoning signals from social and prediction-market data, gated by code, at mid frequency. A Rust engine replays recorded Hyperliquid prices, Kalshi strike ladders and Jev-scored Hacker News posts through one code path, with a risk layer that fails closed.

This page replays recorded data. Paper only: the engine has no order code and sent no orders.

Scored posts

Real Hacker News stories and comments, scored by Jev. Each dot is placed when the score became available to the engine (publication, plus a 60 s poll delay, plus scoring time). Colour is the direction Jev assigned for the selected coin; size is how relevant Jev judged it. Hover for the text; click to zoom the chart there.

Crowd positioning on Hyperliquid (recorded live)

What the 100 best recent traders on Hyperliquid's public leaderboard (by 30-day PnL, account value at least $100k) hold in each coin, read from public wallet state and aggregated: the share of their gross position value that is long. Hyperliquid only exposes current positions, so this exists only where it was recorded live; there is no backfill. Strategy v3 enters only when more than 60% of that value is on its side. No addresses are stored.

The Hyperliquid universe

Results after costs, reported as they came out

Both strategies were written down before any run. On three and a half days of data both lose after costs; the reasoning-gated version loses less, mostly by trading less. That sample is too small to call an edge either way. The engine's job is to find that out fast and safely, and to keep the result whatever it says.

Strategy v4: funding carry across Hyperliquid perps (pre-registered, sealed out-of-sample)

Hyperliquid perpetuals settle funding every hour: when the rate is positive, longs pay shorts that share of position value. v4 ranks a fixed universe of liquid perps by their trailing funding once a day, shorts the coins whose longs pay the most, buys the coins whose longs pay the least, and stays dollar-neutral. It earns funding every hour and pays fees and slippage on every rebalance. The risk is the price leg: crowded longs are often in coins that keep rising.

Decisions and risk blocks

Experiment ledger

Every idea run through mft-engine experiment, appended to a hash-chained log. Each entry carries the hash of the one before it, so a result cannot be edited or dropped without breaking the chain. Kept only if the second-half (holdout) PnL after costs is positive and beats the baseline. Killed ideas stay.

How it works

  1. One event type. Trades, book tops, 1-minute bars, Kalshi ladders, scored posts, top-wallet positioning and detected gaps all become one Rust enum, in time order.
  2. One code path. The same Engine::on_event runs in this replay and live on paper. Only the event source and the clock differ.
  3. The signal. v1 is preregistered 5-minute momentum on 1-minute bars (enter at 2 standard deviations, $1,000 per coin). v2 takes the same entries only when the Kalshi hourly ladder says the price is more likely to close above spot (for a long) or below it (for a short), and lets opposing Jev-scored posts shrink or veto a position. v3 takes the same entries only when the top Hyperliquid wallets hold more than 60% of their position value on the same side.
  4. Reasoning features can only brake. All gates are multipliers between 0 and 1, and a check blocks any order the gates made larger or flipped.
  5. Risk fails closed. Max position, max order, max daily loss, stale data. A rule that errors blocks the order. Every block is logged with its reason.
  6. Honest results. Both strategies lose money after costs on this sample. The sample is a few days, so none of this is evidence of an edge.
